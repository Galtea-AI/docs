## What are Metrics?

Metrics define how Galtea scores your [product's](/concepts/product) outputs during [evaluations](/concepts/product/version/session/evaluation). Each metric captures one specific quality dimension — factual accuracy, tone, security resilience, or a custom criterion you define.

<Info>
  Metrics are organization-wide and can be reused across multiple products. You can link metrics to [Specifications](/concepts/product/specification) for structured evaluation workflows.
</Info>

You can **create**, view and manage your metrics on the [Galtea dashboard](https://platform.galtea.ai/) or programmatically using the [Galtea SDK](/sdk/api/metric/service).

## Two Families of Metrics

- **Deterministic metrics** apply rule-based logic — string matching, numerical checks, bounding box overlap. Results are consistent and reproducible. Built-in examples: [BLEU](/concepts/metric/bleu), [ROUGE](/concepts/metric/rouge), [Text Similarity](/concepts/metric/text-similarity), [Tool Correctness](/concepts/metric/tool-correctness). You can also define your own using the SDK's `CustomScoreEvaluationMetric` class — see [our tutorial](/sdk/tutorials/evaluate-with-custom-metrics).

- **Non-deterministic metrics** use an LLM-as-a-judge to assess open-ended qualities like factual accuracy, misuse resilience, and task completion. Galtea's judges are human-aligned and optimized for each evaluation type. You can also [create your own AI Evaluation metrics](/concepts/metric/evaluation-types#ai-evaluation) with a custom scoring rubric.

## How Metrics Are Evaluated

Every metric has an [Evaluation Type](/concepts/metric/evaluation-types) that determines how scoring happens:

```mermaid

    A[What kind of scoring?] --> B{Automated?}
    B -->|Yes| C{Rule-based logic?}
    B -->|No| D[Human Evaluation]
    C -->|Yes| E[Self-Hosted]
    C -->|No| F[AI Evaluation]
    E -->|Run locally, upload score| H[Pre-computed or CustomScoreEvaluationMetric]
    F -->|LLM judges with your rubric| I[judge_prompt + evaluation_params]
    D -->|Annotators score via dashboard| G[PENDING_HUMAN → score submitted]
```

## Revisions and Analytics

A metric has one active revision. Creating a revision marks the active revision as `Legacy`, also when you start the new revision from an older one. All revisions of a metric share its name, description, tags, documentation URL and evaluation type. A revision created under another name also keeps that name as its own, and the `/metrics` endpoints show it in place of the metric's name. Renaming a revision on the `/metrics` routes renames the metric. In the dashboard, the **Details** tab shows these shared fields on the **Metric** card, and **Edit metric** changes the name, description and tags for every revision. It also edits the specification and user group links of the active revision. The **Revision** card shows the judge settings of the revision you pick, and its **Agreement with human**: 100% minus the average distance between the judge's scores and the human scores, with the number of annotated evaluations behind it. Agreement shows from 10 annotated evaluations. A new revision from the dashboard takes an optional name of its own. To make an older revision the active one again in the dashboard, open it. A yellow band names the revision you are viewing and offers **Mark as active**. The button is hidden for a user who cannot update the metric. For a Galtea metric or a retired judge model it is disabled, and its tooltip names the reason. A **Now active** banner then offers **Undo**, which makes the former revision active again. The dashboard's **Rename revision** item and, in the alpha API, `PATCH /v2/revisions/{id}` instead give that revision alone a name of its own, unique within the metric, and leave the metric's name as it is. A new revision cannot change the evaluation type. Deleting a metric by its ID deletes all its revisions. Deleting a revision deletes only that revision, with its evaluations and specification links, and the metric too when it has no live revision left. You can delete the active revision on its own only when it is the last revision of the metric. In the dashboard, deleting a metric deletes all its revisions.

The **Evaluations** tab lists the evaluations of one revision, the active one unless you pick another. Its **Agreement with human** card shows that revision's agreement with human scores, with the number of its evaluations that carry a human score. Below 10 annotated evaluations it reads **Not enough annotated evaluations**, and its tooltip gives the count. A Human Evaluation metric shows a **Human annotations** card with that count instead. The **Agreement** column shows the same figure for each evaluation, and a dash when it has no human score. Click its header to sort every evaluation that the filters match by agreement, lowest first. Evaluations with no agreement come last in both directions. The tab opens filtered to agreement at most 50%, the evaluations where the judge and the person disagree most. Remove that filter from its pill or with **Remove all filters**. A revision with no human score opens unfiltered. The table shows **Input**, **Output**, **Judge Score**, **Human Score** and **Agreement**. The other columns, such as **Product**, **Dataset**, **Run**, **Status**, **Judge Tokens** and **Token Distribution**, stay in the **Columns** menu.

**+ Compare with** on the same tab picks a second revision of the metric. The tab then lists only the evaluations both revisions judged and a person annotated. Each row shows the input, the human score, one judge score per revision and the change. It marks each row **Better**, **Worse** or **Same**. **Better** means the second revision's judge scored closer to the human score than the first. **Worse** means further from it, and **Same** means both agreements show the same percent. The list opens on **Worse**, the evaluations the second revision gets more wrong. Change the **Change** filter to see the others. The **Agreement with human** card shows each revision's agreement and its number of annotated evaluations, with the difference as a badge when both have an agreement. The **Agreement change** card counts the compared evaluations that got worse, got better or stayed the same. Each revision counts its latest judgment of a case that has a score. The **Judge Tokens** column shows the tokens of the first revision's judge, then the second's. **Token Distribution** shows both bars. Both are in the **Columns** menu. A Human Evaluation metric has no judge to compare.

Legacy and analytics inclusion are separate states. A legacy revision keeps contributing to scores and coverage unless you exclude it. An exclusion applies to one revision: the other revisions of the metric keep counting, and analytics still show one line for the metric. Use it, for example, to drop the results of a bad run on an older revision.

Alpha: the `/v2/metrics` endpoints (`galtea metrics-v2`) take a metric ID. `GET /v2/metrics` lists each metric once, with its active revision's number, name, judge model, evaluation parameters, disabled date, optimization status and analytics exclusion. The active revision also carries its `agreement` with human annotations, its `annotationCount` and `evaluationCount`, and `minAnnotationsForAgreement`, over the evaluations of the products you read. It filters by name, description, tags, evaluation type, creation date, user group, analytics exclusion, and whether Galtea or your organization maintains it. It sorts by `name`, `createdAt`, `agreement` and `annotationCount`, a metric with no agreement comes last when sorted by `agreement`, and a metric with no active revision comes last when sorted by `agreement` or `annotationCount`, in both directions. The `/v2/revisions` endpoints (`galtea revisions`) take a metric ID to list and create revisions, and a revision ID for the other routes: get, rename, export, decision conversion, replay count, analytics exclusion, activation, comparison, and evaluation on annotated cases with its count (`annotated-case-count` and `evaluate-annotated-cases`). `GET /v2/revisions/{id}/comparison?with=<revision ID>` compares two revisions of one metric on the annotated cases both judged, worst changes first, and answers `400` for revisions of different metrics or a Human Evaluation metric. To create a revision from an older one, pass its ID as `parentRevisionId`; by default a revision starts from the active one. A create may also pass a `name`, the revision's own name, under the same rules as a rename: `409` when another revision of the metric, a legacy one included, holds it, `400` for "R" followed by digits other than the revision's own label, and no name stored when it equals the metric's name or the revision's own label. To make an older revision the active one again, call `POST /v2/revisions/{id}/activate` (`galtea revisions activate`) with no body. It marks the current active revision `Legacy`, links this revision to the specifications of the former one in its place, and keeps every evaluation, name and history. The former revision's past evaluations then leave the results of those specifications, such as their analytics and their filtered evaluation list. Activating the active revision changes nothing. It answers `400` for four refusals. The revision belongs to a Galtea metric. The revision is being optimized. The revision is an optimization candidate that nobody applied (failed, declined, waiting for review or with no improvement). The judge model of the revision is retired. It also answers `400` when the metric or the revision changed during the call: reload it and retry. Each revision in `GET /v2/revisions` carries `activatedAt` and `activatedByUserId`: when it last became the active one, and who made it so, by creating it, activating it or applying an optimization. Both are `null` for a revision activated before Galtea recorded them, and a copied revision keeps the time but not the user. Create and `PATCH /v2/metrics/{id}` refuse a name over 200 characters or a description over 5,000 with `400`; `PATCH /metrics/{id}` keeps accepting any length. A route answers `404` when you send the ID of the other kind. Both may still change. The `/metrics` endpoints keep their shape, with five changes. Excluding a revision that is already excluded keeps the first date and user. An ID of another organization answers `404`, like an ID that does not exist, where some routes answered `403`. A lookup by name, such as `names` or an evaluation that names its metrics, checks metric names first, then the own names of active revisions. When two active revisions share that own name, it answers `409`: use the metric ID instead. And a create on `POST /metrics` with `parentMetricId` stores no name on the new revision when the name is "R" followed by digits or another live revision of the metric holds it, and still answers `201`.

The fifth change: a `/metrics` response also carries the revision's `number` (R1, R2, R3) and `revisionName` (its own name, or `null`), while `name` stays the revision's own name when it has one, else the metric's. Evaluations, monitors, monitor alerts, monitor result markers, run metric summaries and metric exports carry `metricRevisionNumber` and `metricRevisionName` beside the metric's name.

After you save a revision, the dashboard shows how many results and products the earlier revision affects. A user limited to the products their user groups grant sees counts that cover only those products, but the exclusion applies to every product. The default keeps those results in analytics. Select **Exclude from analytics** only when you want both the scores and coverage to stop counting them.

You can reverse this decision from the actions menu of the **Revision** card on the metric's page. Exclusion does not delete or change any evaluation. Each evaluation keeps its score, reason, and metric revision.

The metric list shows each metric once, with its active revision in the **Active revision** column. The **Agreement with human**, **Human annotations** and **Evaluations** columns show how the active revision's judge compares with human annotations. Agreement is 100% minus the average distance between the judge's score and the human's, where "Partially correct" counts as 0.5. Below 10 annotated evaluations the cell reads **Collecting data** with the count so far, and a Human Evaluation metric shows none. The list opens on the worst agreement first; **Sort by** also offers the best agreement, the fewest annotations and the name. To inspect earlier revisions, open the metric and pick one in its revision selector. The list hides a metric whose active revision is excluded from analytics. To reveal it, open **Filters** and turn on **Show excluded from analytics**. Add the **Analytics** column from the **Columns** menu to see whether each active revision is `Included` or `Excluded`.

When a product has excluded results, the Details view of its Analytics page states how many results are outside the reported numbers. The link opens the evaluation list with **Show excluded from analytics** active. Excluded revisions do not appear in the Analytics metric filter because they contribute no scores to that page. The page's **Last Successful** date still counts their evaluations, because it reports when an evaluation last ran.

## Pass threshold

Every metric has a pass threshold: the score, from 0 to 1, at or above which an evaluation passes. When you do not set one, it is 0.5. Galtea's own metrics use 0.5. An evaluation with no reported score has no result, so it neither passes nor fails.

You set the threshold when you create the metric. It cannot change afterwards, so to use another one, create a revision of the metric. In the dashboard, the threshold is a percent in **Advanced Options** of the create form, prefilled with 50, and it accepts decimals. A revision starts with the threshold of the revision it comes from. Score pills are green at or above the threshold and red below it.

## Applicability check

Some metrics apply to few sessions. A jailbreak metric, for example, applies only to the sessions where a user tries a jailbreak. If the judge scores every session, it scores many sessions the metric was not made for. Those sessions lower the pass rate or pass for free, and each judge call costs credits.

The applicability check stops this. Before the judge runs, a decision model answers one Yes/No question: does the conversation contain what this metric evaluates? When the probability that it does is below the metric's applicability threshold, the evaluation is skipped. The check runs before a JSON Field Match metric reads a test case's field rules, so a session it skips never reports an error in them. The decision model sees each rule as raw JSON.

A [monitor](/concepts/product/monitor) runs the check for each production session, after the data check and before the judge. See [How a session is evaluated](/concepts/product/monitor#how-a-session-is-evaluated) for the phases and the skip rules.

### Settings

Two settings control the check. Both are fixed when you create the metric. To change one, create a revision of the metric. A revision starts with the values of the revision it comes from, when the check runs for both revisions.

- **Run the check on** (`applicability_scope`) sets which sessions get the check. The options are `PRODUCTION` (production sessions), `DEVELOPMENT` (development sessions), `ALL` and `NONE` (no check). The default is `PRODUCTION`. Development is off by default, because a skip inside a regression suite can hide a test that should run.
- **Minimum certainty** (`applicability_threshold`) is the lowest probability, from 0 to 1, that the metric applies to a session at which the metric is judged. A higher value skips more sessions. The default is 0.5. A session at the threshold is judged. A session below it is skipped.

In the dashboard, both settings are in **Advanced Options** of the create form. Setting **Run the check on** to **Off** hides **Minimum certainty**. The form hides both settings for a metric that has no check. The dashboard shows **Minimum certainty** as a percent from 0 to 100, prefilled with 50. The SDK and the API take a probability from 0 to 1.

The check covers four kinds of metric: AI Evaluation, decision, deterministic, and the DeepEval-based metrics of Galtea. A Self-Hosted, Human Evaluation or G-Eval (deprecated) metric has no check: the scope is `NONE`, and the API refuses any other value. The check never reads traces. A metric that scores `traces` and another field is checked without traces: on each turn's `input` and `actual_output` and its other fields. A metric that scores only `traces` has no check: its scope is `NONE`, and the API refuses any other scope and any threshold. For a decision metric, the fields it scores are the placeholders of its question. Existing metrics got `PRODUCTION` when the check covers their kind, unless they score only `traces`. The others kept `NONE`.

<Note>
  The check runs only where your deployment enables it. Where it is off, every session is judged and the two settings have no effect. On an on-premise deployment, set the environment variable `METRIC_APPLICABILITY_CHECK` of the API to `off` (the default), `shadow` or `enforce`, and set `VITE_METRIC_APPLICABILITY_CHECK_ENABLED` of the dashboard to `true` to show the two settings. The check needs the LLM gateway (LiteLLM) to route to the decision model. Without that route, every session is judged as if there were no check. In `shadow` mode, the check runs and writes what it would skip to the evaluator logs, but every session is still judged. Use it to measure the skip rate of a metric before you enforce.
</Note>

### What the check reads

The decision model reads the `input` and the `actual_output` of each turn, the other fields the metric scores (for example `retrieval_context`, `tools_used` or `expected_output`), and the name and description of the metric. When files are sent to the judge, it also reads the text of each text file on the input and output, and on the retrieval context when the metric scores it. It also reads the metric's criteria: the judge prompt, or the question of a [decision](/concepts/metric/decision-model) metric. For an AI Evaluation metric whose judge prompt names a field, such as tool calls, that field counts as scored. The check never reads traces: one turn's traces can be longer than everything the decision model reads at once. It has no size limit of its own. When a session is too long for the decision model, the metric is judged. Galtea sends this data to the provider of the deployment's decision model. See [Data processing](/concepts/metric/decision-model#data-processing).

The check never blocks an evaluation by failing. The metric is judged as if there were no check when:

- the decision model is not available, or it does not answer in time,
- the conversation is too long for the decision model,
- the probability is below the threshold, but the conversation is longer than about 2,000 tokens. The answer is less reliable on a long conversation, so Galtea does not skip on it.

The check costs no credits.

### A skipped evaluation

A skipped evaluation has no score and no judge call, and it costs no credits. Its error message gives the reason, for example: "The metric does not apply to this session: the probability that it applies is 12%, below its applicability threshold of 50%."

A retry would ask the same question about the same session, so `canRetry` is `false`. When the skip is a session's latest evaluation in its metric family, the session stays out of that family's count in the monitor's alert rules, unless a reviewer scored the evaluation. See [Alert rules](/concepts/product/monitor#rule-types). Analytics still count the skip in its skipped total.

For a metric that applies to few sessions, raise the monitor's sampling percentage, so that enough sessions reach the check.

To correct a skip, use one of these:

- A reviewer scores the evaluation. The [human score](/concepts/product/version/session/evaluation#param-human-score) turns the skipped row into a scored one.
- Create a revision of the metric with **Off** as the scope, and evaluate the session again with it.

## Optimization review

**Optimize** writes an improved judge prompt into a new revision of the metric, with the metric's name. While it runs, that revision's page shows its progress, and you can leave the page. Unlike other revisions, it does not mark the original `Legacy`. When the optimizer returns a different prompt, the revision shows **Ready for review**, and its page shows the changes and both prompts.

- **Apply optimization** marks the original revision `Legacy`. The optimized revision joins the original's specifications. It already shares the metric's name, description, tags and user groups. Monitors use it from their next run.
- **Keep original** leaves the original unchanged and marks the optimized revision **Not applied**. You can then optimize again.

The optimizer judges each prompt at the metric's pass threshold, and the optimized revision keeps that threshold.

A metric runs one optimization at a time. An optimization that fails stays in the revision history as `Failed` and can never score, and you can start another at once.

To optimize a metric, a user needs both `metric.create` and `metric.update` in their user group lists, because **Apply optimization** and **Keep original** each need `metric.update`, and while an optimization waits for review, you cannot optimize the original again. You also cannot start a new revision from the optimized revision while it is running or waiting for review. A result that nobody settles keeps blocking the next optimization of the original.

From 30 annotated evaluations, a run keeps some of them as a validation set. The optimizer does not train on the validation set. An evaluation kept for validation stays out of training in later attempts of the same metric. The **Optimize** confirmation shows how many evaluations go to the training set and how many to the validation set. A user limited to the products their user groups grant sees counts that cover only those products. With fewer than 30 annotated evaluations, there is no validation set unless an earlier attempt kept one. Optimize is disabled when the evaluations kept for validation leave too few to train on.

Expand **Advanced Options** in the **Optimize** confirmation to narrow a run. **Strategies** sets which rewrites the optimizer tries: **Fix false passes**, **Fix false failures**, **Add a scoring rubric**, **Simplify the prompt** and **Broaden the wording**. Each step asks for one rewrite per selected strategy and keeps the best one. **Maximum steps** sets how many steps the run may take, from 1 to 10, and defaults to 10. A run stops earlier when the prompt stops improving. With every strategy selected and 10 steps, the run is the same as one without options. The review card, the No improvement notice and the page of a declined or applied revision show the steps the run used out of its maximum and the strategies it ran. An attempt that did not report them shows **Not reported**.

The review compares the original and the optimized prompt on both sets. For each prompt, it shows how many of the evaluations that both prompts scored agree with your annotation, and the alignment when it is defined. A declined or applied revision keeps these results on its page. The validation result is evidence, not a guarantee: a small validation set gives a rough estimate.

Only the active revision of a metric can be optimized. Through the API, call `POST /metrics/{id}/activate-optimization` or `POST /metrics/{id}/decline-optimization` with the optimized revision's id.

## Evaluator model retirement

The evaluator model of a metric is its LLM judge, or the decision model of a [decision](/concepts/metric/decision-model) metric. When an evaluator model is retiring, you get an email up to 30 days before the retirement date. It names the affected metrics and the evaluator models you can use instead. It offers only models of the same kind as the retiring one: judge models for a judge model, decision models for a decision model. The email goes to the organization owners and admins, and to the creator of each affected metric.

A metric's evaluator model cannot change after you create it. For your own metrics, create a new revision with another evaluator model.

Galtea maintains its own metrics, so you cannot revise them. If your organization used one in the last 90 days, its owners and admins get the email too. Galtea may replace one of its metrics with a new revision on another evaluator model. When it does, the email names the new revision and links to it. Use that revision to keep evaluating. When there is no new revision, create your own metric with one of the listed evaluator models to keep evaluating after the retirement date.

If the evaluator model becomes unavailable to Galtea on or after the retirement date, and stays unavailable for six hours, the same recipients get one email naming the metrics that stopped scoring.

## Evaluator model in an EU-only organization

In an organization with the [EU-only data region](/security/data-region), a metric whose judge model has no EU deployment is unavailable. A judge that Galtea has not checked yet keeps running. A decision metric follows the same rule through its decision model. An unavailable metric shows as **Unavailable**, and the API gives the reason in `unavailableReason`. Its evaluations are skipped. Create a revision with another judge to run it. You cannot revise a Galtea metric, so use it as a template for a metric of your own with another judge. See [Judge models in an EU-only organization](/security/data-region#judge-models-in-an-eu-only-organization).

</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Pass Threshold" type="Number" optional>
  The score, from 0 to 1, at or above which an evaluation of this metric passes. Defaults to 0.5. It is fixed when you create the metric: create a revision to change it. In the SDK it is `metric.pass_threshold`. **Example**: 0.7
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Applicability Scope" type="Enum" optional>
  Which sessions get the [applicability check](#applicability-check): `PRODUCTION`, `DEVELOPMENT`, `ALL` or `NONE`. Defaults to `PRODUCTION` for a metric the check covers, and to `NONE` for a Self-Hosted, Human Evaluation or G-Eval (deprecated) metric. A metric that scores only `traces` never gets the check: it defaults to `NONE` and refuses any other value. It is fixed when you create the metric: create a revision to change it. In the SDK it is `metric.applicability_scope`. **Example**: "PRODUCTION"
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Applicability Threshold" type="Number" optional>
  The lowest probability, from 0 to 1, that the metric applies to a session at which the metric is judged. Defaults to 0.5, and reads `None` in the SDK until you set it. A metric with no check refuses it: a Self-Hosted, Human Evaluation or G-Eval (deprecated) metric, or one that scores only `traces`. It is fixed when you create the metric: create a revision to change it. In the SDK it is `metric.applicability_threshold`. **Example**: 0.3
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Tags" type="Text List" optional>
  Tags for categorization. **Example**: ["RAG", "Conversational"]
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Evaluation Type" type="Enum" required>
  How outputs are scored: AI Evaluation, Human Evaluation, or Self-Hosted. See [Evaluation Types](/concepts/metric/evaluation-types) for full details.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="User Groups" type="List[String]" optional>
  [User groups](/concepts/user-group) for Human Evaluation metrics. Controls which annotators can score evaluations for this metric.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Evaluation Parameters" type="List[Enum]" required>
  The data fields available to the evaluator. See [Evaluation Parameters](/concepts/metric/evaluation-parameters) for the full reference. Not applicable for Self-Hosted metrics. For a decision metric, the platform derives them from the placeholders in the question.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Decision Question" type="Object" optional>
  The one Yes/No or Scale question a decision metric asks. Only for the decision source. See [Decision model](/concepts/metric/decision-model).
</ResponseField>

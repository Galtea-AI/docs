## What is a Product?

A product in Galtea represents a functionality or service that you want to evaluate. Examples include _QA chat_, _Summarization_, or _Content Generation_.

<Note>Even if your organization has a single public-facing product (like a website or dashboard for all user interactions), you should define separate Galtea products for each distinct functionality you want to evaluate.</Note>

You can **register**, view and manage your products on the [Galtea dashboard](https://platform.galtea.ai/). Products can also be viewed and managed programmatically using the [Galtea SDK](/sdk/api/product/service).

## SDK Integration

The SDK allows you to list existing products, retrieve specific products by their ID or name, and delete products. Product registration is done through the [Galtea dashboard](https://platform.galtea.ai/). See the [Product Service API documentation](/sdk/api/product/service) for more details.

## Product Properties

<ResponseField name="Product Name" type="Text" required>
  The name of your product.
  **Example**: "Document Summarizer"
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Product Description" type="Text" required>
  Write what the product must do and what you want to test or evaluate: what it must get right, what it must refuse, and where it must be accurate.
  <Info>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>What to include:</strong> The behaviors that must hold, the limits the product must respect, and the results that must be correct.</li>
      <li><strong>What to avoid:</strong> A marketing summary. Concrete behavior produces specific specifications; a summary produces generic ones that would fit any AI product.</li>
    </ul>
    <br/>
    Learn how to write effective product descriptions in our <a href="/sdk/tutorials/writing-specifications">detailed guide</a>.
  </Info>
  <strong>Example:</strong> "A mobile sales assistant for smartphones. It must recommend only phones that are in stock, never quote a price it cannot find in the catalog, and hand over to a human when the customer asks about an existing order."
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Capabilities" type="Text" required>
  List what your product is designed to do and what information it has access to. This helps Galtea generate targeted test cases.

    <strong>Example:</strong> "Can answer questions about phone specifications, recommend devices based on user preferences, can access past user purchases, compare prices across models, and provide information about warranties and accessories."
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Inabilities" type="Text" required>
  List what your product cannot do — information it does not have access to, tools it cannot use, and its general scope boundaries. This prevents generation of irrelevant or misleading test cases.

    <strong>Example:</strong> "Cannot process purchases, access customer account information, or provide technical support for device repairs."
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Policies" type="Text" optional>
  Define specific rules and instructions for how the product must behave in certain situations. Policies ensure consistent, safe, and compliant responses.
  <Info>
    Policies consist of clear instructions, such as "**always** answer with **x** if asked about **y**" or "add **[specific disclaimer text]** at the end of your responses when asked about **z**."
  </Info>
    <strong>Example:</strong> "If asked for guaranteed investment returns, respond that all investments carry risk. Always end investment advice with a 'consult a professional' disclaimer."
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Risk Level" type="Enum" optional>
  The risk level of the product under the EU AI Act.
  Possible values:
  - `GPAI`: General-Purpose AI Model (Article 3(63), Title VIII A - Chapter 1).
  - `GPAI Systemic`: General-Purpose AI Model with Systemic Risk (Article 52a, referring to criteria in Article 52c).
  - `Special System`: AI Systems with Specific Transparency Obligations (Article 50).
  - `High`: High-Risk AI System (Article 6, Annexes I & III).
  - `Prohibited`: Prohibited AI Practices (Article 5).
  <Info>Legal definitions available in the [Rules for trustworthy artificial intelligence in the EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng).</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Operator Type" type="Enum" optional>
  The operator type of the product under the EU AI Act.
  Possible values:
  - `Authorised Representative`: An entity established in the EU, appointed by a provider from a third country, to act on their behalf regarding AI Act obligations.
  - `Deployer`: An entity that uses an AI system under its authority, except when the AI system is used in the course of a personal non-professional activity.
  - `Distributer`: An entity in the supply chain, other than the provider or importer, that makes an AI system available on the Union market.
  - `Importer`: An entity established in the EU that places on the market or puts into service an AI system bearing the name or trademark of a person established outside the Union.
  - `Product Manufacturer`: The manufacturer of a product that incorporates an AI system.
  - `Provider`: An entity that develops an AI system or that has an AI system developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark.
  <Info>Legal definitions available in the [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/).</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Auto Close Scope" type="Enum" optional>
  Which sessions Galtea closes automatically after they stay inactive too long. Set it through the API; the SDK only reads it.
  Possible values:
  - `NONE` (shown as **None**): never close sessions automatically.
  - `DEVELOPMENT` (shown as **Simulated sessions**): only close simulated sessions.
  - `PRODUCTION` (shown as **Real sessions**): only close real sessions.
  - `ALL` (shown as **All sessions**): close both simulated and real sessions.
  <Info>The API default is `ALL`. See [Simulated and Real sessions](/concepts/product/version/session#simulated-and-real-sessions).</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Auto Close Inactivity Minutes" type="Number" optional>
  How many minutes a session can stay inactive before Galtea closes it automatically.
  <Info>The API default is `30`.</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="OpenTelemetry Product Key Values (otelProductKeyValues)" type="Map" optional>
  One value for each attribute of the product key, for example `service.namespace` and `service.name`. An OpenTelemetry trace whose resource carries these values, and Galtea fact attributes (or attributes named in the version attributes) but no `galtea.product.id`, goes to this product. Two live products of one organization cannot hold the same values. Set it in **Advanced Options** of the product edit form, or with `POST /products` or `PATCH /products/{id}`. See [Route Traces to a Product by Resource Attributes](/sdk/tutorials/monitor-real-user-traffic-with-opentelemetry#route-traces-to-a-product-by-resource-attributes).
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="OpenTelemetry Product Key (otelProductKey)" type="List of text" optional>
  The resource attribute names that identify this product, 1 to 10 names. Leave it `null` to follow the product key of the organization. Set it to give this product its own key.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="OpenTelemetry Version Attributes (otelVersionAttributes)" type="List of text" optional>
  The resource attribute names whose values make up the facts of a version, for example `service.version` and `app.agent.model`, 1 to 32 names. Leave it `null` to follow the version attributes of the organization, and then it follows every later change. Set it to give this product its own list, which replaces the organization list. A product list cannot be empty. An OpenTelemetry trace that carries no `galtea.version.id` and no `galtea.version.fact.*` attribute goes to the version whose facts these attributes make up. Set it in **Advanced Options** of the product edit form, or with `POST /products` or `PATCH /products/{id}`. See [Name the version](/sdk/tutorials/monitor-real-user-traffic-with-opentelemetry#name-the-version).
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="OpenTelemetry Input Source Rule (otelInputSourceRule)" type="Object" optional>
  Reads the input of each turn from a named non-LLM span, for a user message that no LLM span carries. It has four parts: `spanName` (the exact span name), `attribute` (the span attribute that holds the JSON), `path` (a JSONPath to the text, starting with `$`), and an optional `predicate` that applies the rule only when a value matches. Leave it `null` to follow the rule of the organization. A version rule overrides this one. Set it in **Advanced Options** of the product edit form, or with `POST /products` or `PATCH /products/{id}`. See [Recover the Turn Input from a Non-LLM Span](/sdk/tutorials/monitor-real-user-traffic-with-opentelemetry#recover-the-turn-input-from-a-non-llm-span).
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="OpenTelemetry Output Source Rule (otelOutputSourceRule)" type="Object" optional>
  Reads the output of each turn from a named non-LLM span, for a reply that no LLM call produced. It has the same four parts as the input source rule. Leave it `null` to follow the rule of the organization. A version rule overrides this one. Set it in **Advanced Options** of the product edit form, or with `POST /products` or `PATCH /products/{id}`. See [Recover Replies That Do Not Come from the LLM](/sdk/tutorials/monitor-real-user-traffic-with-opentelemetry#recover-replies-that-do-not-come-from-the-llm).
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Closed Session Inference Creation Policy" type="Enum" optional>
  What happens when you try to add an inference to a session that is already closed.
  Possible values:
  - `Reopen And Reevaluate` (the default): keep the inference and open the session again. The session's monitor evaluations move to the `Outdated` status, so a [Monitor](/concepts/product/monitor) scores the session again once it closes for good. A simulated session reopens the same way and only stores the turn, because it has no monitor evaluations.
  - `Reject`: refuse the inference and return an error.
  - `Ignore`: silently drop the inference.
  <Info>The API default is `Reopen And Reevaluate`.</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Interaction Mode" type="Enum" optional>
  Who consumes the product's output.
  Possible values:
  - `Conversational`: a person exchanges several turns with the product.
  - `Human Direct`: a person reads the output directly, with no dialogue.
  - `Programmatic`: another system consumes the output and no person reads it directly.
  <Info>The onboarding AI pre-fills a suggestion. Change it or clear it at any time in the dashboard or through the API; the SDK only reads it. An unset value means the product is unclassified. The dashboard also lays out a session's turns from this value. `Conversational` and an unclassified product show a User and Assistant dialogue. `Human Direct` and `Programmatic` show an Input panel above an Output panel.</Info>
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="Primary Task" type="Enum" optional>
  The one dominant task the product performs on its input.
  Possible values:
  - `Conversational Assistance`: helps a person through a dialogue.
  - `Question Answering RAG`: answers questions from retrieved documents.
  - `Document Extraction`: pulls structured fields out of a document.
  - `Document Generation`: writes a new document from instructions or data.
  - `Summarization`: shortens a longer input while keeping its meaning.
  - `Classification`: assigns the input to one of a fixed set of labels.
  - `Translation`: renders the input in another language.
  - `Correction`: fixes errors in the input, such as grammar or code.
  - `Agentic Workflow`: plans and runs several steps or tools to reach a goal.
  - `Other`: none of the tasks above.
  <Info>The onboarding AI pre-fills a suggestion. Change it or clear it at any time in the dashboard or through the API; the SDK only reads it. An unset value means the product is unclassified.</Info>
</ResponseField>

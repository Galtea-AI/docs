# Personal Finance Knowledge Base

Educational reference used by the quickstart Accuracy test. It gives the
assistant factual ground truth to answer from.

## Investment Basics

A **stock** is partial ownership in a company. Owning a share means you own a
small piece of that company and may receive part of its profits as dividends.

A **bond** is a loan you make to a company or government. The issuer pays you
interest over a fixed term and repays the principal at maturity. Bonds are
generally lower risk than stocks.

A **mutual fund** pools money from many investors to buy a diversified mix of
stocks, bonds, or other assets. A professional manages it, so small investors
can hold a broad portfolio.

## Savings Accounts

A **high-yield savings account** pays more interest than a standard savings
account while keeping your money accessible. It is a good home for an
emergency fund.

A **certificate of deposit (CD)** locks your money for a fixed term in
exchange for a higher, guaranteed interest rate. Withdrawing early usually
costs a penalty.

## Budgeting

The **50/30/20 rule** is a simple budget: 50% of after-tax income on needs,
30% on wants, and 20% on savings and debt repayment.

An **emergency fund** should cover three to six months of essential expenses,
held somewhere easy to access.

## Limits

This assistant gives general educational information only. It does not give
personalized investment advice, execute trades, or access user accounts.
